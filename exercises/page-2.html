Purple Rain

Introduction

Purple Rain is a song by American musician Prince and his backing group, The Revolution. It was released in 1984 as the title track of the album Purple Rain. That album also served as the soundtrack to the film of the same name. The song became one of Prince's signature performances and remains closely associated with his musical career.

Origins

Prince first developed Purple Rain with a country-music sound in mind. He considered making it a collaboration with singer and songwriter Stevie Nicks, but the song eventually developed into the dramatic rock ballad audiences know today. Prince connected the image of purple rain with the end of the world, love, and the importance of faith during difficult times.

Recording

The song was recorded at the First Avenue nightclub in Minneapolis on 3 August 1983, during a benefit concert for the Minnesota Dance Theatre. Prince performed with The Revolution in front of a live audience, and the recording became the basis for the released track. The concert is remembered as a significant event in Prince's career and in the city's music history.

Reception and legacy

Purple Rain has appeared on several influential lists of popular music. An earlier Rolling Stone ranking placed it at number 144 among the 500 greatest songs, while Q magazine recognised it among notable guitar tracks. Pitchfork also selected it as its top song of the 1980s. Its emotional singing, extended guitar solo, and memorable live performances have contributed to its lasting reputation.
